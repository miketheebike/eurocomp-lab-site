Why evaluate

In a world of scarce resources, you need evidence on what is actually worth funding

The EU is one of the world’s largest investors in innovation, yet significant regional disparities in innovation outcomes are limiting its overall competitiveness. To remain on par with global counterparts, every euro invested needs to yield greater returns and have a more substantial impact on growth.

The processes behind these investments often lack mechanisms for continuous learning, even as policy needs to keep adapting to evolving circumstances. This calls for identifying challenges, testing solutions, and refining approaches.

This is what we call science-based adaptive delivery: testing, learning, and adjusting as you go.

Testing and learning only works if you know what to look for

When evaluating projects, we often only assess outputs, because they are the easiest thing to count. Impact evaluation is what lets you move from measuring outputs to proving outcomes.

Outputs

What a program delivers: grants disbursed, firms enrolled, trainings run.

Outcomes

What changes because of it: growth, jobs, innovation, resilience.

To understand in advance why each step should lead to the next, you need to map out, before implementation, how a program’s activities are expected to change the problem you are addressing. That causal logic, the how and why behind each step, explains a program’s reason for existing, and tells you what to measure to test whether the expected change is actually happening.

What is an impact evaluation

But what is impact evaluation exactly?

It allows us to answer the questions: what impact did this intervention have on regional development, and how did different approaches change the outcome? For whom did it work best, and under which conditions?

To answer that, an impact evaluation isolates a program’s actual effect from everything else going on (economic trends, other policies, or the kinds of firms likely to apply in the first place), rather than just describing what happened. It compares what actually happened after introducing a program to what would have happened without it, the counterfactual.

Not the same as M&E

M&E tracks whether a program is implemented as planned. Impact evaluation tests whether it caused a real difference, compared to what would have happened without it.

Built in from day one

The comparison group, sample size, and data collection all depend on decisions made before a program launches, not added on afterward.

Each one builds on the last

Each evaluation answers some questions and raises new ones, helping you understand your regional market and design more impactful programs next time.
