Spain

The problem, and the programme

The problem

Market failures keep research and development (R&D) investment in Extremadura below its optimal level. Small and medium firms in the region under-invest in innovation, particularly in agriculture and the ecological transition.

What was done

The Regional Government of Extremadura (JUNTA) and the Extremadura Science and Technology Park Foundation (FUNDECYT-PCTEx) created an EU-funded matching grant programme for small and medium firms.

Each cycle awards €3.5 million in grants, up to €250,000 per firm (€138,000 on average), financing 35% to 80% of a firm’s proposed R&D investment.

What experimentation sought to answer

“What is the impact of R&D grants on SMEs’ business performance?”

Spain: financial support to innovate

The programme
Matching grants of up to EUR 250,000 per firm (average EUR 138,000) for R&D projects
Where
Extremadura, Spain; regional government (JUNTA) with FUNDECYT-PCTEx, EU-funded
Who is randomized
Firms: SME applicants, mainly agriculture and ecological transition, scored on 11 criteria
The design
Bin-ranking RCT: the middle bin randomized, 22 funded vs. 34 not funded
Outcomes tracked
Private R&D investment, implementation success, expected sales growth
Starting point
Market failures keep regional R&D investment below its optimal level

Applications
External scoring
Middle-bin lottery
R&D grants
Follow-up survey

How the programme was tested

Extremadura ran an advertising campaign to attract at least 100 applicants. External reviewers scored every application on 11 objective criteria and grouped proposals into low, medium and high quality.

Why this design. Strength: it leverages investment-return uncertainty for applications with unclear returns. Limitation: it requires a large sample size in the middle range (bin).

Randomisation successfully balanced key firm characteristics: the comparison group and the treatment group looked the same on average. There was no evidence of firms with certain characteristics dropping out of the study more than others (selective attrition) in follow-up survey responses.

What the programme achieved

Three months after receiving the grant, firms in the treatment group reported higher planned R&D investment and stronger confidence in their projects than the comparison group.

+48%

€65,000 above the comparison-group average.

Probability of successful implementation

Up from 16% without funding, according to expert priors.

Expected sales growth, conditional on success

From 8% to 16% after receiving the grant, according to expert priors.

Over half of the comparison-group firms planned to continue their R&D projects using other funding or their own resources. Public R&D subsidies can significantly boost private investment, but careful targeting is needed to avoid simply displacing money firms would have spent anyway.

Czechia

The problem, and two pilots to solve it

The problem

Entrepreneurial activity remains low in Czech regions hit by the decline of the coal industry. These regions need to diversify away from carbon-intensive industries and grow their private sector.

In September 2022, the European Commission approved the Just Transition Programme (JTP): €1.641 billion for business R&D, post-mining regeneration, skills, clean energy and the circular economy in the Moravian-Silesian, Ústí and Karlovy Vary regions.

A key part of the JTP is a voucher scheme for entrepreneurs: up to €21,100 for business development, up to €42,200 for innovation, and up to €21,100 for digital transformation.

What was done

The World Bank tested two light-touch, complementary pilots that add skills support on top of the voucher scheme.

Smart Start (Moravian-Silesian region): seven workshops teaching a scientific approach to decision-making under uncertainty, covering risk versus uncertainty, hypothesis testing and A/B testing, aimed at voucher recipients and other interested firms.

AI Klub (Ústí and Karlovy Vary regions): five hands-on workshops on using AI for efficiency, marketing, sales, product development and financial management, with peer-to-peer exchange between firms.

What experimentation sought to answer

“Does light-touch support to SMEs improve firm capabilities and performance?”

Croatia

The problem, and the programme

The problem

Firms and scientists in Croatia struggle to collaborate: information frictions, uncertain transaction costs and limited outreach budgets get in the way, especially for small and medium firms.

Only 8% of Croatian firms collaborate with higher education institutions, far below the EU average. A World Bank survey found that 47% of firms lacked information about relevant scientists.

What was done

The World Bank, the Innovation Centre Nikola Tesla and the Ministry of Science, Education and Youth designed “Scinergy”, a business-science matchmaking pilot targeting fields such as AI, energy and biotechnology.

Matched firms and scientists took part in a two-day matchmaking event in February 2024: “speed-dating” sessions followed by joint workshops, plus legal and communication support to help pairs plan R&D projects together.

What experimentation sought to answer

“Does connecting firms with scientists foster research partnerships and innovation?”

Croatia: matching businesses with scientists

The programme
Scinergy: matchmaking events, workshops, and ongoing support for firm-scientist pairs
Where
Croatia; Innovation Centre Nikola Tesla with the Ministry of Science, Education, and Youth
Who is randomized
210 eligible firms, from over 250 applications, seeking scientific collaboration
The design
Simple lottery: 100 firms invited to Scinergy vs. 110 not invited
Outcomes tracked
Collaboration attempts and partnerships, innovation, R&D employment
Starting point
Only 8% of Croatian firms collaborate with higher education, far below the EU average

How the programme was tested

Firms applied between November 2023 and January 2024. In February 2024, eligible firms were randomly assigned by lottery to receive matchmaking support or not.

Why this design. Strength: a lottery among applicants ensures wide outreach, thanks to a simple application process. Limitation: firms may drop out after applying, which can reduce statistical power.

What the programme achieved

+38%

Collaboration attempts with scientists

Treated firms were more likely to attempt and to sustain collaborations with Croatian researchers.

+50%
