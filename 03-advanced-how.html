Do I have enough firms?

Randomization only works if there are enough firms to detect meaningful differences.

Too few firms

Real impacts may go undetected: ordinary differences between firms drown out the programme’s effect.

Enough firms

Real impacts become measurable: averages settle and the difference stands out.

More firms do not make the lottery fairer; they make its answer visible.

Why sample size matters

How many firms are enough depends on what needs to be detected.

The size of the effect

Small effect: more firms needed.

Large effect: fewer firms needed.

The variation between firms

High variation: more firms needed.

Low variation: fewer firms needed.

Think of trying to hear a quiet voice in a noisy room: more background noise requires more observations.

What if there are not enough firms?

The most common planning problem has practical fixes.

Increase outreach

More applications mean a larger pool. SMART Romania ran events in more than 20 cities.

Expand eligibility

Loosen the bar so more firms qualify.

Combine regions

Pool similar programmes or areas into one study.

Run a phase-in

Everyone participates; timing creates the comparison.

Target larger effects

Focus the programme where impacts are biggest.

Collect better baseline data

Good baselines reduce noise and cut the required sample.

Which outcomes should be measured?

An RCT is only as good as what it measures.

Good outcomes are

  • Relevant: tied to the policy objective
  • Measurable: data can actually be collected
  • Objective: not dependent on opinion
  • Consistent: collected the same way for every firm, every round

Measured in SMART Romania

  • Digital adoption (DESI)
  • Revenue
  • Employment
  • Productivity

Fix the outcomes before the draw: choosing them afterwards invites cherry-picking.

What can go wrong?

Even a well-designed RCT must survive contact with reality.

Attrition. Firms drop out of surveys, and the groups stop matching.

Spillovers. Comparison firms benefit anyway, and the measured difference shrinks.

Low take-up. Treated firms never actually use the support.

Implementation problems. The programme is delivered unevenly across firms.

When are the alternative designs worth it?

How introduced four alternative designs. The advanced question is when they earn their extra sample and complexity.

Multiple treatment arms

Choose it when: knowing which version works best matters more than sample size.

Phase-in

Choose it when: everyone must eventually receive the programme.

Randomized intensities

Choose it when: spillovers between firms or employees are the question.

Encouragement

Choose it when: access cannot be denied and take-up is the problem.
